NeurIPS 2025 in San Diego was my first time at the conference, and the first thing that struck me was its size. It brought together people from academia, industry and policy, with sessions on everything from new methods to the effects of AI on society.

Large language models, multimodal systems and reinforcement learning were everywhere. At the same time, much of the discussion was about safety, ethics and societal impact, which reflects a growing awareness of the responsibility that comes with building these systems.

![Tesla exhibit at NeurIPS](/images/blogs/neurips-2025-tesla.jpeg)
*The Tesla demo area on the expo floor.*

The expo floor showed both sides. Companies had polished systems on display, while the conversations around them kept coming back to basics: data, alignment and robustness. Some of my best conversations were short ones with strangers, about trade-offs, open problems, and work that did not go as planned.

![Photo with Geoffrey Hinton](/images/blogs/neurips-2025-hinton.jpeg)
*A quick moment with Geoffrey Hinton.*

One moment I will remember is meeting Geoffrey Hinton. I have followed his work for years, so even a brief meeting felt surreal.

I left inspired, with a better sense of how broad the field has become and how much of its progress depends on people from different communities talking to each other.
